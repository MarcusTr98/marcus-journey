Train-to-Role: Standardizing Role-based Training

Manufacturing Kaizen · 2019–2020 · Redesign of onboarding training and deployment

PDCAStandard workTraining designQC toolsLean
−53%Classroom training time
−80%On-line practice time

Project overview

Shift from general training to role-based learning, with clear work standards and skill verification.

Problem

New hires trained across multiple roles, although their assigned work required only one task group.

Current state

Complex tasks received too little practice during classroom training.

Root cause

Training was not grouped by role or required work standard.

Countermeasures

Assign roles early, group learning content, standardize instructions, and assess skills.

Improvement results

The simulated scenario reduces classroom training time by 53% and on-line practice time by 80%.

Sustain

Review regularly, update standards, and assess post-training performance.

Project role

Proposed the improvement, designed forms, analyzed data with QC tools, and tracked PDCA.

Fishbone · training not aligned to roles

The cause branches converge on one issue: training content was not designed for each role.

5 Whys · tracing the root cause
  1. Why 1 · Why was more time needed to become proficient?
    →
    Answer 1

    After classroom training, new hires still needed additional line practice.

  2. Why 2 · Why was additional practice needed?
    →
    Answer 2

    Complex tasks for the role were not practiced enough in initial training.

  3. Why 3 · Why were complex tasks not practiced enough?
    →
    Answer 3

    Training time was split across several different roles.

  4. Why 4 · Why cover content from multiple roles?
    →
    Answer 4

    The program was shared and not grouped by actual work.

  5. Why 5 · Why was training not grouped by work?
    →
    Answer 5

    There was no role-orientation step or set of role-specific work standards.

Root cause: no role-based training path with matching standards and skill assessments.
